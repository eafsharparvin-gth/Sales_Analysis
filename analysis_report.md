# 1- Executive Summary

This project analyzes 100 sales orders to evaluate overall 
sales performance, profitability, product performance, 
customer behavior, salesperson performance, warehouse performance,
category performance, and monthly trends.

The dataset generated a total revenue of 7,125.74 and 
a total profit of 1,550.42, resulting in an overall 
profit margin of 21.75%. Across the 100 orders, 
a total of 1,229 units were sold.

The average order generated 71.25 in revenue and 15.50 in profit,
with an average of 12.29 units sold per order.

Overall, the analysis indicates that the business generated 
a positive profit margin, while differences in product, customer, 
salesperson, warehouse, category, discount, 
and monthly performance provide useful opportunities for 
further business optimization.

# 2- Data Preparation

The dataset consisted of multiple related tables, including Orders, Products, Customers, and Salespersons.
These tables were combined to create a unified dataset named master_df, which was then used for the analysis.

The data preparation process included:

Inspecting the structure and data types of the tables.
Checking for missing values and duplicate records.
Converting Order_Date to the appropriate datetime64 data type for time-based analysis.
Joining the related tables using their corresponding identifiers.
Using Unit_Price from the Orders table and Unit_Cost from the Products table.
Calculating key derived fields such as Total_Amount and Profit.
Verifying the calculated values before proceeding to KPI analysis and visualization.
After the preparation process, the resulting master_df contained 100 orders and 20 columns, providing a unified dataset
for the subsequent business analysis.

## 10- Time-Based Analysis

The analysis examined four key monthly indicators:

* Monthly Revenue
* Monthly Profit
* Monthly Orders
* Monthly Quantity Sold
The results showed that June was the strongest month in terms of both revenue and profit in the analyzed dataset. Monthly order volume and quantity sold were also compared to identify differences in sales activity across the analyzed months.

The monthly visualizations provide a clearer view of changes in business performance over time and can help identify stronger and weaker periods for future sales and operational planning.

## 11- Discount Analysis

The relationship between discount levels, revenue per order, and profit margin was analyzed to evaluate the impact of different discount levels on sales performance and profitability.

The average revenue per order for each discount level was:

    Discount    Average Revenue Per Order
    0%          73.12
    5%          82.00
    10%         73.89
    15%         54.58
The results show that increasing the discount did not consistently lead to higher revenue per order. The 15% discount level had the lowest average revenue per order, while the highest average revenue was observed at 5% discount.

Profit margin showed a similar pattern:

    Discount    Profit Margin
    0%          21.50%
    5%          23.03%
    10%         22.16%
    15%         21.10%
The 15% discount level also had the lowest profit margin. Therefore, higher discounts did not demonstrate a clear improvement in either revenue per order or profitability in this dataset.

These findings suggest that discount policies should be evaluated carefully rather than assuming that larger discounts automatically generate better sales or profitability.

## 12- Relationship Analysis

Scatter plots were used to explore relationships between key numerical variables and identify potential patterns in the sales data.

The analysis of Quantity Sold versus Profit showed a generally positive tendency: larger order quantities were often associated with higher profit values. However, the relationship was not perfectly linear, and the data showed considerable dispersion.

As quantity increased, profit values became more widely distributed, with observations appearing across both lower and higher profit levels. This indicates that order quantity alone does not fully explain profitability and that other factors, such as product cost, selling price, category, discount, and product mix, may also influence profit.

Therefore, the relationship should be interpreted as an observed pattern rather than a direct causal relationship.

## 15- Business Recommendations

##### **1** - Review Discount Strategy

The 15% discount level was associated with the lowest average revenue per order and the lowest profit margin. Discount policies should therefore be evaluated based on their actual impact on revenue and profitability rather than assuming that higher discounts will increase sales performance.

##### **2** - Identify and Share Top Sales Practices

Daniel achieved the highest revenue and profit among salespersons. His sales patterns and performance can be examined to identify practices that may be useful for improving the performance of other sales team members.

##### **3** - Focus on High-Margin Categories

Dried Fruits achieved the highest average profit margin at 30.95%. Management can further evaluate this category in terms of demand, pricing, product availability, and potential for expansion.

##### **4** - Investigate Warehouse Performance

W001 achieved both the highest revenue and the highest profit margin. Operational factors such as inventory availability, product mix, order processing, and warehouse efficiency can be reviewed to identify practices that may contribute to its stronger performance.

##### **5** - Analyze Monthly Performance Differences

June showed the strongest revenue and profit performance. The factors behind this performance should be investigated, such as product mix, customer activity, discount levels, or sales performance, to determine whether similar conditions can be replicated in weaker months.

##### **6** - Use Multiple KPIs for Decision Making

Revenue alone does not provide a complete picture of business performance. Future decisions should consider revenue, profit, profit margin, order volume, quantity sold, and average order value together to avoid focusing on sales volume at the expense of profitability.

##### **7** - Expand the Analysis with More Historical Data

The current analysis is based on 100 orders and a limited time period. Adding more historical data would make it possible to identify stronger seasonal patterns, monitor performance over longer periods, and produce more reliable business recommendations.
